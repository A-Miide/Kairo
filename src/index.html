Help & Guide

How Kairo works, start to finish. Click a section to expand it, or search for a word.

Getting started

Kairo listens to whoever's preaching, matches what's said against the Bible in real time, and puts the right verse on screen automatically — the same way a slide operator would, without someone driving it live.

  1. Open Settings → Audio and pick the microphone (or audio-routing device, e.g. BlackHole) capturing the preacher. The level bar under it shows what that input is delivering; play something through it to check. Kairo evens out a quiet or loud feed on its own, and the line beside the bar says if the source is clipping.
  2. Open Settings → Outputs and turn on at least one destination — usually External Display for the projector/screen.
  3. Check Settings → Language: Kairo starts in this computer's language, says what that language gets, and offers anything it needs.
  4. Press Start Listening. Live Status switches from Idle to Listening, and the Live Transcript Log starts filling in as words are heard. Stop ends the session — nothing else does: if the internet drops, the status shows Reconnecting… and Kairo keeps listening; the words said meanwhile are caught up when it's back.

Everything else — playlists, slides, songs, themes — can be prepared before or during the service; detection runs independently in the background the whole time.

Cameras, layouts and the live stream are there when you want them: see Director mode and Streaming below.

Live scripture detection

A verse reaches the screen on its own in three ways — nobody needs to press a button:

  • A citation is spoken — "Romans 8:28", "Romans chapter eight verse twenty-eight", "the 23rd Psalm", "first Corinthians 13 verses 4 to 7", "verse 28 of Romans 8", "verses 4, 5 and 6". A citation corrected in the same breath ("John 3:17 — sorry, 16") ends on the corrected verse. When a book name or verse number is misheard as another real one — Ephesians/Philippians through an accent, "First John" heard as "John", "verse 53" for 63 — what the preacher says just before and after decides, and the screen switches to the verse those words fit.
  • A book and chapter are named without a verse ("turn to Matthew 6") — the words that follow are matched against that chapter's verses to find the one being read.
  • Scripture is read aloud, cited or not — the spoken words are matched against the Bible text, in the KJV or the church's translation (Settings → Bible translation). Verses that read almost the same (Matthew 6:33 / Luke 12:31) are told apart by the words only one of them has.

Once a passage is up, the preacher can move within it: "verse 5", a number said on its own, "next verse" / "the following verse", "previous verse", "go back to verse 3", "the last verse". A spoken range ("verses 1 to 9", "from verse 4 to the end") advances verse by verse as the reading continues.

Kairo also follows a verse put in the preacher's own words ("the Bible says don't worry about anything, pray about everything…"). A paraphrase goes to the screen on its own only when every check agrees; otherwise it waits in Possible Matches. Modern wording counts too: a verse quoted from memory the way the NIV or NLT puts it ("plans to prosper you and not to harm you") is offered even when your church reads the KJV. Well-known passages named in passing ("the prodigal son", "the Lord's Prayer") are offered in Possible Matches too.

Possible Matches holds what Kairo found but isn't certain enough to put up on its own — click one to send it. This is deliberate: a wrong verse on screen is worse than a missed one. While a song or slide is live, only a spoken citation goes up on its own; lyrics that quote scripture are offered in Possible Matches instead.

Singing and interpreters. While the room is singing, the Live Transcript Log shows (music) and lyrics are never sent on their own, even when they quote scripture. When a service is interpreted, the interpreter's turns show as (interpretation) and only the preacher's words are used for detection.

Asking for a translation out loud. When the preacher asks for a version — "John 3:16 in the NIV", "Isaiah 7:7, NLT", "switch to the New King James" — the verse on screen changes to it and the translation select flashes. It lasts for that passage: reading on stays in it, and the next verse from another passage goes back to the church's translation. Choosing a translation yourself always wins. A translation that isn't installed (the Message) is ignored, and simply mentioning one ("the NIV says…") never switches.

To decide every verse yourself, switch Auto off (the Auto-Deploy badge, or Put confident matches on screen automatically under Settings → Bible): nothing goes to the screen by itself, and every detected verse waits in Possible Matches for a click (in Director mode, on Preview for Send).

The Live Queue marks the verse on screen right now with LIVE. If a wrong verse goes up — or something is cleared by mistake — press Back (the arrow by Clear Bible, or ⌘←) to put back what was showing before; Kairo won't immediately send the undone verse again. In the Live Transcript Log, a chip marks the words that sent each verse — click it to send that verse again.

Finding a verse yourself

Use the search field at the top of the Bible tab:

  • Type a reference — "Jn 3:16", "Ps 23:1-3", "Romans 8" — and press Enter to send it (a range or chapter goes up verse by verse).
  • Type a few words of a verse — from the third word, matching verses appear right under the field, even if the words are out of order or in your own words ("renew strength eagles", "cast your cares on him"). One click sends it to the screen; the arrow keys and Enter work too, Escape closes the list.

The list only shows verses the words actually identify, so a phrase every other verse contains ("the Lord is") shows nothing until you type a little more.

Press / to jump to the search field. Double-click a line in the Live Transcript Log to search those words — the quickest way to find a verse Kairo missed.

Searching everything (⌘F)

Press ⌘F (Ctrl+F on Windows), or choose Edit → Find…, from anywhere in Kairo to search it all at once: Bible verses, songs, every playlist item and its slides, media files, timers and this Help.

  • Results are grouped by kind. What the search most clearly names comes first: a reference ("Jn 3:16", "Ps 23") puts the Bible first, a song or playlist item's title puts those first. Each group shows its best few; Show all lists the rest.
  • ↑ ↓ move through the results, Tab jumps to the next group, Esc closes.
  • Enter opens a result where it lives (its tab, scrolled to it) and never changes the screen. ⌘Enter, or the Send / Add button on the row, does what clicking it in its own tab does: a verse, slide or media file goes live, a song opens Add to playlist.

With nothing typed, the box lists your recent searches.

Building a service

The left sidebar is your run-of-show — a playlist of items in the order they'll go out. Each top tab is a different kind of content to add:

  • Bible — search and add specific verses directly; also where Live Status, the transcript log, and Possible Matches live during a service.
  • Slides — your own slide decks (announcements, sermon notes, anything not scripture).
  • Songs — hymns and lyrics, with section labels (Verse/Chorus/Bridge) and line-by-line/section navigation.
  • Timer — countdowns for service segments, sent to the output's own timer layer independently of whatever slide/verse is showing.
  • Media — images and video, sent to their own independent media layer (doesn't interrupt or get interrupted by slides/verses).

Use + New in the sidebar to create additional named playlists (e.g. one per service) — Default is just the one that's there from the start.

Click any slide in an item to send it live; the currently-live slide is highlighted. Right-click a slide for more options (duplicate, delete, split, etc.).

Quick edit (right-click a song or deck) has two ways to edit. Slides gives a text field per slide: Shift+Enter splits a slide at the caret, and Backspace at the very start joins it onto the one before. Text shows the whole song or deck as one piece of writing, the easiest way to paste in or rework lyrics: a blank line starts a new slide, and a line like [Chorus] or [Verse 2] starts a part (in a deck, it names the next slide). The slides it makes are listed beside the text as you type, and each slide keeps its own theme.

While Kairo is listening, slides follow along by themselves: a live song or sermon deck advances as it's sung or preached (the bar under the playlist shows Following and where it is), and a slide deck in the playlist comes up on its own when its first slide is said. When the slides move on while a scripture is on screen, the slide takes the screen back — the preacher has moved on. A slide that is itself scripture (the sermon's verses put on slides) is left to Bible detection, which puts the verse up as it's read. Click any slide to take over — following carries on from there. Stop listening and it stops.

Importing content

Bring in existing material instead of rebuilding it by hand, from either File → Import or the + button on a playlist item:

  • Import… opens the Import window, which lists every format Kairo reads: ProPresenter (.pro/.pro6/.probundle/.proplaylist, plus .protheme themes), PowerPoint (.pptx), Word (.docx) and text (.txt/.md). Pick a row, or drop a file on the window.
  • Paste from clipboard — pasted lyrics/text, split into slides automatically.

ProPresenter files are handled specially. Kairo auto-detects them and preserves the file's own design exactly as authored — fonts, colors, positions, images, layer structure — instead of reformatting it into a Kairo theme. The Add dialog shows a notice confirming this rather than asking you to pick a theme, since none is needed. A playlist file with several presentations imports all of them at once, each keeping its own original layout.

A plain text/Word import instead asks how to split it into slides (by paragraph, or a fixed number of lines each) and which theme to render it with, since there's no original design to preserve.

Theme Studio & looks

Theme Studio is where a slide's actual design lives — background, text position/font/color, animations. A theme applies to every slide in an item that doesn't have its own override.

A ProPresenter-imported slide is the one exception: it carries its own preserved layout directly (see Importing above), independent of whichever theme is assigned to the rest of the item — editing it in Full-Scale Edit adjusts that one slide's own layers, not a shared theme.

The Multi-Language theme is a special split layout — the verse's own language on one side, a translated version on the other (see Multi-Language below).

Monitor layouts design what a Monitor output shows for whoever is up front. Start from New → New monitor layout or edit one of the two under Monitor layouts. A text layer's Shows can be what is on screen now, its reference, the next slide, the time of day, the countdown or the message from the booth; add backgrounds, logos and shapes like any theme. Pick it for a Monitor under Settings → Outputs (Layout); Built-in keeps the standard view. Monitor layouts never show up as themes for slides or verses.

Director themes (Director mode only, under Screens) are whole-screen designs for layouts: a background, frames and logos, a Video zone where the camera shows and a Content zone where slides and Bible verses show, each in its own theme. Add the zones with the two tools next to Image, or start from New → New Director theme. Put a verse text layer in a Director theme and it styles the verse itself instead of using a content zone.

Sending to outputs

Settings → Outputs lists every destination on the left; click one to see and edit its settings on the right. Use + Add Output (top right) to add a Display/Projector, an NDI output, or a Syphon output — each new one can be renamed and given its own theme. The switch in each output's header (list row or detail panel) turns that destination on or off; a dot next to it shows whether it's actually connected/broadcasting.

  • External Display — a real window sent to a projector/screen/monitor. Enable at least one; it's what most services actually need. If it doesn't come back after restarting the app, reselect it here — this should happen automatically on launch, but a manual reselect always forces it.
  • NDI — broadcasts over the network to any NDI-aware app (OBS, vMix, ProPresenter, etc.), on any platform. Requires the free NDI Tools runtime installed on this machine first (ndi.video/tools) — Kairo doesn't bundle it. If the toggle won't turn on, the output's status line will say why (usually "NDI runtime not found").
  • Syphon (macOS) / Spout (Windows) — same idea as NDI but same-machine only, and bundled with Kairo (nothing extra to install). Any Syphon- or Spout-aware app on the same computer (OBS, ProPresenter, Resolume, etc.) can pick it up directly. The Add Output menu only offers the one that matches the computer Kairo is running on.
  • OBS — a lightweight built-in text push into a single OBS Text Source, for a quick lower-third without adding an NDI/Syphon source in OBS. For full parity (images, video, timer, not just verse text), add an NDI or Syphon source inside OBS pointed at Kairo instead — that's the recommended setup.
  • ProPresenter — there's no separate Kairo-side integration for it. Add Kairo as an NDI or Syphon source directly inside ProPresenter, the same way you would in OBS.

The Monitor (right side, under Clear Bible) shows exactly what each output is showing right now. Pick Main or any output that's on from its list, or use the grid button to see them all side by side — click a tile to watch just that one. The arrow button opens the monitor in its own window, about the size of Settings: resize or move it as you like; it stays above Kairo while you keep working.

Each NDI/Syphon output also has its own configurable resolution and network/source name, editable any time under its detail panel.

Output Control (further down the same page) has one card per output with a switch for each kind of content: Scripture, Slides and songs, Timer, Media, Overlays, and Words only. Each card ends with one sentence saying what that output shows and what is hidden. Layout gives one display a Layout of its own; its theme takes over from the display's, and None gives the display its own theme back. A Monitor always shows words only, so Media is dimmed there.

Output layers, bottom to top: the background (the theme's, or a layout's in Director mode), Media (background image or video), Slide, Bible, the timer, and Overlays always on top. Output Control shows this order too. A Words only or keyed output shows the words alone: no media, overlays or layout frame. When a scripture goes up — called by the preacher or sent by you — it covers the live slide without ending it, and your view stays where it is. Slides that follow along while listening keep the slide underneath up to date. Clear Bible (lit while a scripture covers a slide) brings that slide back; Clear Slide removes only the slide; clicking a slide yourself sends it straight out.

Background music and overlays

Background music (Settings → Audio → Sound output) plays on its own while slides, scripture and video change. Add sound files with + Add music, then play them there or from the small player that appears under the Monitor. Choose what happens after a track (stop, repeat all, repeat one), a fade length, whether the music lowers under a video with sound, and whether it starts when Kairo opens. Play on picks the sound device. Remote controls (OSC) can play, pause, stop, skip and fade it out.

Overlays (Settings → Overlays) are pictures and videos that sit on top of everything on the screens, like a logo, a "Live" badge or a lower-third. Add any number with + Add overlay (png, jpg, gif, webp, svg, mp4, webm, mov). Switch each on or off, drag it in the preview or use the sliders for size, position and opacity, and choose whether it shows when Kairo opens. Sets group overlays, for example one for a special service: switch a whole set on together without touching the others. Which outputs show overlays is set under Output Control.

Director mode

Turn on Settings → Director mode to switch Kairo to a screen for cameras and inputs. The normal screen comes back when you turn it off.

  • Inputs — your cameras and capture cards, as a grid of tiles or a list. Add them under Settings → Video Sources; an input can be a named placeholder before the device is connected. Click an input to stage it on Preview; double-click puts it on air.
  • Preview and Output — Preview is what is staged, Output is what is live. Under them: Send (Space), Cut or Fade with the fade length, Fade to black and Undo.
  • Layouts — saved setups for Preview: the input, which layers are on, the overlays and a theme. Click one to load it into Preview, then Send. Alt+1 to 9 load the first nine. A Layout's theme is drawn around the slide or verse: the slide or Bible verse keeps its own theme and branding inside it, unless that theme has its own verse text. Customize a Layout's theme in Theme Studio (Video zone and Content zone tools) from the menu on a Layout.
  • Remote — Stream Deck and OSC can drive Send, Fade to black, Undo, Layouts 1 to 9 and Inputs 1 to 9.
  • Menus — the Director menu has Send to Screen (⌘Return), Fade to Black (⌘⇧B), Undo Last Send, Inputs and Layouts 1 to 9 (Inputs 1 to 4 on ⌘⌥1–4). The Production menu has Go Live, Recording, Run Macro and Auto-send.
  • Keys — 1 to 9 put an input on air, Shift + 1 to 9 stage one on Preview, Alt + 1 to 9 load a layout, Space sends, Esc clears what is staged, ⌘Z (Ctrl+Z) undoes the last send. The ? button lists them all. Ready check lists anything not ready (an input with no device, a missing camera) before the service.
  • Leaving Director mode puts the normal screen back as it was: the outputs' layers and overlays a layout changed are restored, the layout frame goes, and every camera is released.
  • Scripture sends — search, the Bible, Theme, the verse transition and the Live Queue in one place. The Auto switch decides whether detected verses go straight to the screen or wait on Preview for Send.
  • Top menu — Media, Songs, Timer and Slides open as a card over Director, with a Done button (Esc or a click outside also closes it). The cameras keep running.
  • Bottom half — the playlist, and the media bin with toggles for Slides, Songs and one for each media folder. A click stages a slide or media item on Preview; Send (or a double-click) puts it on the screen. Right-click a slide to edit it.
Streaming

Settings → Streaming is where the destinations for a live stream are kept: YouTube, Facebook, Twitch or any RTMP or RTMPS address, each with its own switch, so one stream can go to several places. Pick the video size, frame rate and quality your internet upload can carry, and the audio quality. Audio always comes from the input chosen under Settings → Audio.

Go live (top bar, or on that page) starts every destination that is switched on; the button then shows LIVE and how long you have been on air, and each destination shows whether it is live. Click it again to stop. The stream is exactly what the output shows: scripture, slides, the layout, overlays, and in Director mode the camera on air, placed in the layout's video zone; switching cameras or layouts never drops the stream. If the connection drops, Kairo reconnects by itself.

When there is more than one output, each destination can stream a different one (Streams in its settings). Check tests that this computer can reach a server without starting anything. Stream keys stay on this computer. Stream Deck, OSC and macros can start and stop the stream and recording.

Recording: Record (top bar in Director mode, or the Record button under Settings → Streaming → Recording) writes what the output shows to a file on this computer, in Movies → Kairo unless you choose another folder. Under Recording you choose the format (MP4, MOV or MKV), the size, the frame rate and the quality; while you are streaming, the recording is the stream's own picture, at the stream's size and quality. It is separate from going live: record without streaming, or stream without recording. The file is written as it goes, so a recording cut off by a crash or power cut still plays up to that moment; if something goes wrong mid-way, Kairo carries on in a second file beside the first instead of writing over it.

Sound mixer: under Settings → Streaming → Sound, the stream and recordings can take several inputs at once (say the desk feed and a room microphone), each with its own level, mute and meter. While live or recording, the Sound button in the top bar opens the same mixer; levels and mutes change straight away without interrupting anything. Double-click a fader to put it back to 0 dB.

Sound: by default the stream takes the input chosen under Settings → Audio. Use your sound desk's stream or aux feed for it (through a USB audio interface or the desk's own USB): it already carries the microphones, the band and Kairo's music as the room hears them. Kairo doesn't add its own music on top, so nothing is heard twice. With no desk, a loopback driver (such as BlackHole on a Mac) can combine a microphone and Kairo's sound into one input.

Macros

A macro does several things with one press. Make one under Settings → Macros: give it a name and add steps, for example load Layout 2, switch the welcome overlay on, start the music, wait 3 seconds, Send. A step can be anything Stream Deck or OSC can do, a message on the Monitors or the screens, an overlay on or off, or a wait.

Run it from the Macros button in the top bar (it appears once there is a macro), or from Stream Deck and OSC: the first nine are /kairo/macro-1 to /kairo/macro-9, in the order shown in Settings. Clicking a running macro stops it before its next step. A macro sends nothing a person couldn't: a Send step sends what is in Preview, as the Send button would.

Multi-language

Settings → Language is set up in two places. 1 Listening: pick what the preacher speaks. Three lines say what that language gets (heard, by Deepgram or offline; verses found; which Bible they show in), and the offline speech model it needs is offered right there. 2 Bible language: which Bible verses come from. It follows the preacher, and every Bible comes with Kairo. Optionally, a second language shows verses and slides in two languages at once on the Multi-Language theme; its slide translator downloads when you pick it.

For a guest minister, switch the language from the chip beside Start Listening, and switch it back after.

For a service preached in Spanish, Portuguese, French or German, set the service language and Kairo understands citations in that language ("Juan capítulo tres versículo dieciséis", "siguiente versículo") and matches verses read aloud against that language's Bible. Other languages are transcribed, and verses are found when cited or read in English.

Service languages (Settings → Language): English (US, UK, Australia, India), Spanish, Portuguese (Brazil), French, German, Italian, Dutch, Swedish, Polish, Ukrainian, Russian, Hindi, Indonesian, Korean, Japanese, Chinese (Mandarin), or Auto-detect. Deepgram covers all of them. Offline, English uses the English model (about 465 MB) and the others the offline model for other languages (about 475 MB): only the one the preacher's language needs is offered.

Bibles that come with Kairo, all offline: English — KJV and NLT, plus NIV, ESV, NASB and NKJV (Settings → Bible translation) — and, under Bible language, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and Swahili (New Testament only). These change what a verse is shown in; what Kairo listens for is what the preacher speaks. In any language but English, the English translation setting doesn't apply.

Church vocabulary (same page) — names and words your services use that transcription may mishear: the pastor, the church, local places. One per line; used from the next Start Listening (English and auto-detect services).

Slide Translation Models are separate — they translate ordinary (non-scripture) slide/sermon text for the Multi-Language split theme, using a small bundled local model with no setup, API key, or internet connection required once downloaded.

Troubleshooting
  • Nothing is being detected — confirm the right microphone/audio device is selected under Settings → Audio and that Live Status shows Listening, not Idle.
  • The transcript skips words or stops while the preacher is talking — open Settings → Audio and read the line beside the level bar. Clipping: the source is too loud before it reaches Kairo; turn it down at the desk or player. Silent: nothing is reaching that input (wrong input selected, or the source is muted). A quiet feed is raised automatically. When testing with a video on this computer through BlackHole, the player's own volume sets Kairo's input level.
  • A verse is correct but never appears live — check Possible Matches; a lower-confidence detection waits there for a manual click rather than auto-sending.
  • The output window is blank or on the wrong screen — reselect it under Settings → Outputs → External Display.
  • An NDI output won't turn on — the output's own status line shows the exact reason. The first time, Kairo offers to download NDI's free runtime from NDI (one click; on Windows, one permission prompt). Installing NDI Tools from ndi.video works too.
  • An imported file lost its look — only ProPresenter files preserve their own design automatically; a plain text/Word import always uses a Kairo theme instead, by design.
  • A slide disappeared when the preacher cited a verse — the verse covers the slide; the slide is still live underneath. Press Clear Bible (lit while this is the case) to bring it back, or it comes back by itself when the slides follow the preacher on to the next one.
  • Something odd happened in a live service — Kairo keeps a log of what it heard and did in server.log, in its app-data folder (on a Mac: ~/Library/Application Support/com.kairo.scripture). Send it along with what you saw.
  • Live Status says Reconnecting… — the internet connection dropped. Kairo keeps listening and reconnects by itself; words said in the meantime are caught up. Nothing to do.
  • A name keeps being misheard (the pastor, the church) — add it under Settings → Language → Church vocabulary.
  • A citation's book or verse number comes through wrong ("Philippians" for Ephesians, "John" for First John, 53 for 63) — Kairo switches once the words around it show which verse is meant. If the preacher only names the verse and moves on, nothing tells them apart: send the right one from Possible Matches or the search box.
  • A stream destination says Reconnecting — that server dropped the connection; Kairo keeps trying while the others stay live. If it stays that way, check the key with the service (a key is often only valid for one event) and use Check on the Streaming page.
  • A camera is missing from the stream — the Streaming page says which camera couldn't be opened; it is left out so the stream keeps going. Close any other app using it, then stop and go live again.
  • Go live says no FFmpeg can stream — install a current FFmpeg (on a Mac: brew install ffmpeg), or set KAIRO_FFMPEG to one.
  • A camera light stays on after leaving Director mode — that shouldn't happen; quit and reopen Kairo, and send server.log (see above).
